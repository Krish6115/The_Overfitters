Day 2: Once we identify what actually works, we stop randomly exploring and focus on improving those approaches. We can try better models, tuning, preprocessing, loss functions, or post-processing only where the experiments justify it.

Day 3: No major new ideas unless absolutely necessary. Focus on ensemble, inference optimization, reproducibility, final validation, documentation, and submission checks.

Since we are only 3, we should avoid duplicating work. Let’s maintain one master notebook and a shared experiment log. Everyone can experiment separately and then we integrate only the approaches that show a measurable improvement.

Also, let’s not blindly chase the public leaderboard. The private leaderboard is what matters, so our validation strategy and reproducibility are more important than a temporary public LB boost.

Finally, let’s keep the implementation efficient because the dataset is large. We shouldn’t immediately jump to huge models. Start with a reliable baseline, understand where it fails, and then introduce complexity only when there is evidence that it helps.

Our goal for the 72 hours: understand → baseline → parallel experiments → select promising approaches → refine → ensemble → reproduce → submit.

Since we’re 3 people, communication is going to be critical. Every time someone gets a result, post what you changed + validation metric + training time + what you learned, not just “this model is better.”
